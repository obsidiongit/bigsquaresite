## What transfers to our industry template

- **The skeleton itself**: hero with answer paragraph, differentiators, franchise-angled service links, one educational block, one deep case study, industry FAQ, CTA. Same arc as their homepage (our section-order source) tuned to one audience.
- **Answer paragraph naming sub-verticals in the hero**: doubles as the GEO answer block and seeds the sub-market keywords high on the page.
- **Two-CTA repeat**: the identical pair appears 3+ times (hero, why-choose, mid-band, close). Already our 6.1 pattern.
- **Service blocks with industry-specific angle + link**: every deliverable line is franchise-flavored, never the generic service pitch. This is the anti-thin-content move D4 warns about, proven in the wild.
- **One case study told deep** (challenges then results) beats a card grid on an industry page. Maps to our gated proof slot: when real data exists, feature ONE story with substance plus the index link.
- **FAQ questions are industry-specific** ("How does franchise SEO differ...", "What makes a franchise website stand out"), not buyer-process repeats. Matches our D5 split.

## What we do NOT take

- The photo-ground hero and frosted card (our hero is type on paper; media only when real assets exist).
- **The stamping slip, the most instructive frame in the capture**: the ROI table's closing line reads "you can expect more qualified **plumbing** leads" on the FRANCHISES page (07). Cross-page debris from stamping without a per-page copy pass. Our per-page copy-pass rule exists precisely for this; keep the screenshot as the cautionary exhibit.
- Their check-glyph density (five lists in two viewports). We enumerate with the 6.10 numbered ruled list, capped per section.
- Invented-adjacent claims and badges (Google Premier badge, "2k+" avatar chip). Copy-rules bans all of it without sources.
- ALL-CAPS-free zone: their headline case is fine, but everything else about the skin (orange, DM Sans, WordPress widgets, WORK WITH US sticker) stays theirs.
