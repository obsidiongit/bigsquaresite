# Obsidion Portal Design Brief

v3, 2026-08-25 (Brad's portal session). Replaces v2, which was written before the region pivot and still specified GridLines rails, the 1200 Container, a Nº label, and a 12-column split. Everything below FeaturedWork now runs the open region (STYLE_GUIDE 4.5), so the section is rebuilt in that system.

Position on the page: **directly after Services**, before the testimonial. Build phase 2F, its own session: this is a flagship differentiator, give it room.

Role: the transparency pitch. "Most agencies send a slide deck once a month. We give you a login." The section should feel like being handed the instrument itself.

## Decisions locked with Brad, 2026-08-25

1. **The window is a structural mock, no numbers.** Full app chrome built in HTML and CSS, with every value rendered as a neutral skeleton bar instead of a digit, plus a visible preview chip. Honest at a glance, and swapping in real screenshots or live code later is a data change, not a layout change. Validated by paper.design's own placeholder state (`../reference-images/paper-design/paper-home-desktop-06.png`).
2. **Composition order: copy, window, features, CTA.** The window is the centerpiece of the section, not a closing illustration.
3. **The cube reforms into the window.** The companion's journey no longer ends at the services dock: it re-materializes here and becomes the portal. See "The set piece" below.
4. **Ground: tint.** Keeps the page's light run, and a `--paper` window on a `--surf` ground gives the exhibit its lift without a second dark block so soon after the proof band.

## The window

An Obsidion web-app shell, `aria-hidden` illustration throughout. The copy carries every claim; nothing inside the window is a claim.

- **Object treatment**: `bg-paper`, `--radius-media` (24), 1px `--sec-line` border, the 6.11 resting shadow, inset inside the `EDGE` width. `RegistrationMarks` at its corners (our vocabulary's answer to paper.design's selection handles). Generous air above and below: the gap is what makes it read as an exhibit.
- **Chrome**: a browser or app shell, **never macOS traffic lights** (Obsidion is a portal a client logs into, not a desktop app: see the reference analysis "what we do not take"). Top bar carries the Obsidion mark, the account row, and a tab strip.
- **Left rail**: the portal's own nav, one item lit.
- **Body**: the sections the five features name, as structure: a metric row, a channel breakdown, a lead table, a spend pacing block.
- **Values**: skeleton bars only. No digits, no client names, no dates, no axis labels, no percentages. Repeated neutral row labels are allowed where a real UI would repeat them (paper.design's "Album name" pattern), as long as they name a field and never a value.
- **The preview chip**: mono outline chip pinned inside the window's top-left, reading `PORTAL PREVIEW`. Must stay legible at every width. This is the decisions.md honesty gate.
- **A second, smaller window** overlapping the main one's lower left for depth (the paper.design steal 3): a compact alert or call-log card. Same placeholder discipline. Desktop only.
- **Swap contract**: the window's content is data-driven from one module so a real screenshot, a screen recording, or live embedded code can replace it without touching the section's layout.

## The set piece: the cube becomes the portal

The companion cube currently dissolves over the services exit (`HomeCanvas` WAYPOINTS, `anchor: "services"`, `frac: 0.99`, `fade: 0`). That dissolve is retired: the cube now carries through into this section and turns into the window.

Reuses the proven architecture from the featured work panel morph (`lib/work-panel.ts`, 2b rounds 5-11) rather than inventing a second mechanism: a **pin plus runway**, with two actors reading one shared clock (`lib/portal-window.ts`), the canvas cube and the DOM window, meeting at a deterministic square-slab handoff.

Beats:

1. **Reform.** The cube fades back in as the section enters and travels to the window's spot, spinning down.
2. **Dive and flatten.** It flattens to a square slab of a fixed viewport-height edge, exactly as the work morph does (uniform bevel, no corner distortion).
3. **The handoff.** The DOM takes over from the slab. This is where the portal beat must differ from the work panel beat, so the page does not repeat itself. **Built better than specced (2026-08-25): the blue square the cube flattens into IS the Obsidion mark in the window's chrome bar.** The cube dives onto the mark's exact measured box, and the DOM's seed square shrinks from the slab's edge onto the mark's, sitting on top of the real one when it retires: an invisible swap rather than a fade. The chrome bar stretches out around it to the frame's full width, then the body unrolls downward like a screen coming on. The object does not become a panel, it becomes the software's identity, which no other beat on the page does.
4. **Settled.** The window is a normal DOM object. The cube is gone; it became the product.

Rules carried over, non-negotiable: `clip-path: inset(... round Npx)` for the stretch so radii never distort; the canvas slab never stretches; a checkpoint band covers only the committed slice of the runway so a park can never leave a half-built window; reduced motion and no-WebGL render the settled window with no runway at all (the pin collapses).

Because this replaces the services dissolve, the services waypoints need their exit retimed rather than faded, and the cube's fade-out at the section's far edge is no longer needed at all: the window is the ending.

**Build order for the session**: the static section first (composition, window, features, CTA) verified at all four widths and reviewed with Brad, then the set piece. The window has to be right as an object before it is worth animating into.

## Build note, static section (2026-08-25)

`Portal.tsx`, `PortalWindow.tsx`, and `lib/obsidion-preview.ts` shipped; the section sits directly after `Services` on the homepage. Verified on the prod build at 375/768/1280/1536 plus reduced motion: no horizontal overflow at any width, the preview chip renders at every width, **zero digits anywhere inside the window** (probed, the honesty gate holds), zero running animations at rest, typecheck and build green. Console shows only the known Phase 4 service-link prefetch 404s, now including `/services/obsidion-portal/` from this section's CTA.

Deviations from the spec above, all deliberate:

- **The chip sits at the right end of the window's top bar**, not pinned top-left. Top-left collides with the Obsidion mark inside the frame and with the registration mark outside it, and putting the chip in the bar also removes any need for a fake account row. It is more prominent there, not less.
- **Progressive disclosure inside the window** rather than one fixed crop: the left rail and the lead table appear at `lg`, the budget pacing panel at `lg`, the tab strip at `sm`, and the second window at `xl`. Below those the window narrows to the top bar, the metric row, and the channels panel, which still reads as the product without squeezing a 4-column table into 375.
- **The two-tone headline is two `BaselineReveal`s**, an `h2` for the statement and a `p` for "Live." at the same size in `--sec-mid`. The primitive splits plain text by design, and an `h2` wrapping block children would be invalid markup.
- **The window's shadow rides an outer wrapper** around `ClipReveal`, because ClipReveal clips its own overflow and would otherwise eat a shadow cast from inside it.
- The sparkline in each metric tile is **bars, not a line**, so it can never be read as a trend against an axis.

## Build note, the set piece (2026-08-25, same session)

Shipped after Brad approved the static window and confirmed the real portal arrives as LIVE CODE, not screenshots. That call changed the architecture before the morph was built: `PortalWindow` is now a SHELL (frame, chrome bar, Obsidion mark, preview chip) with the body as a SLOT that `PortalPreviewBody` merely fills by default, so live code drops in without touching the exhibit treatment, the layout, or the morph, whose target lives on the shell.

New: `lib/portal-window.ts` (the shared clock, beats, checkpoint band, and `seedGeometry`), `components/sections/home/PortalStage.tsx` (the pin), `components/sections/home/PortalExhibit.tsx` (the DOM actor plus the exhibit treatment on every path). `HomeCanvas` gained the `portal` anchor, its approach waypoints, the portal stage state, and the cube's dive/flatten/flood beats; **the services-exit dissolve is retired**, so the companion no longer fades out at the end of its journey.

Findings worth keeping, all caught by probing the real build:

- **A pin must not hold content that has nothing to show yet.** Pinning the copy together with the exhibit parked a clipped line of support text over an empty viewport for the first two thirds of the runway, because the window does not exist until the handoff. Pinning the exhibit ALONE fixed it: the copy scrolls away like any section's, and the cube inherits the window's still-empty footprint (already framed by its registration marks) to travel and turn in. At a 900px viewport the copy and a full-height window cannot both clear the nav anyway.
- **The clip's first phase paints before the handoff.** A growing `inset()` always reveals something, so a sliver of chrome bar sat under the travelling cube. The frame needs its own opacity gate on the handoff crossfade, exactly as the work panel's DOM panel has.
- **`clip-path` clips shadows too.** The frame's shadow has to ride an outer element and arrive with the settle, or it is invisible for the whole morph and then pops.
- **A chrome bar the same colour as the section ground disappears when it is the only thing on screen.** The bar was `bg-surf` on a tint (surf) section: fine once the paper body was under it, invisible during the stretch beat. The bar is now `bg-paper` and the lit tab chip carries the tint instead.
- **The flood must complete BEFORE the handoff, not at it.** The DOM seed is flat `--acc`; a cube still 20 percent glass at the crossfade shows a step in colour.
- **The cube must hold LARGE here.** It travels the page at scale 0.4 to 0.46; turning at that size in an otherwise empty viewport read as a loading spinner, not a set piece. The portal hold is 0.58, the biggest it has been since the hero, and it compacts into the slab on the dive.

Verified on the prod build: the runway scrubs 1:1 under real wheel input at 1280 with the beats landing in order (frame and seed both dark until p 0.56, bar stretching from the seed at 0.60 to 0.71, seed retired onto the mark by 0.75, body unrolled by 0.95, clip dropped at 1); the services exit hands the cube over with no seam, arriving beside the headline; 375/768/1280/1536 plus reduced motion all settle correctly (runway 0 at 375 and under reduced motion, 1260px at 768 and up), no horizontal overflow anywhere, zero running animations at rest, zero digits inside the window, the chip visible at every width, no page errors, typecheck and build green.

Probe note for future sessions: `window.scrollTo` cannot scrub these runways. Lenis lerps programmatic scrolls, and the checkpoint band glides any programmatic park to its edge. Drive `page.mouse.wheel` in small steps instead, which is real input and reproduces exactly what a visitor's finger does.

Open with Brad: the beat timings and the hold's scale are first-pass and belong to his 2K retune along with the rest of the journey; the runway is 140svh; whether the section wants the copy back on screen during the morph (it would mean a shorter window).
